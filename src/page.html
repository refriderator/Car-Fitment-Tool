What the numbers mean

  • Poke / tuck is how far the outermost part of the tire or rim, in the top third of the wheel, sits outside or inside the fender lip at the top of the arch.
  • Tire to lip is the smallest gap between the tire and the lip, measured only where the tire sits under the lip (from the lip edge to 22 mm inboard). Bump to lip is how far the wheel can travel up before that gap closes.
  • At lock is the smallest tire-to-lip gap while the front wheels sweep from full left to full right at this ride height.
  • The red line on the car is the lip the tool measures to. It is found from the 3D body, so flares move it.
  • Not checked: the inside of the wheel well, the strut or coilover, the brake caliper against the spokes, and the arms. The wheel wells in these 3D files are not true to the real cars. Use "inner vs factory" to judge the inside.
  • The wheel face is a flat picture. Spoke shape, concavity and caliper clearance are not modelled.
  • Camber and the lower arm are set by hand. Camber does not change with ride height here, and the tire is not squashed by load.
  • Tire width on a rim other than its measuring rim is a rule of thumb: about 5 mm per half inch of rim width.

Where the cars come from

  • Bodies are the 3D files you supplied, with wheels, brakes and interiors removed and the mesh thinned for the browser. They are placeholders: each is one particular build, not a factory car.
  • Hub faces are placed from the factory track and factory offset. That is an assumption: a modified car may run longer arms or different hubs.
  • The ride height each page opens with is my choice so the starting wheels clear. The pose stored in each 3D file is shown under Stance.
  • Pivot heights for camber and steering come from each file's physics data. The IS300 file models the front as a strut; the real car has double wishbones.
